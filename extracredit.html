🎨 Nancy Baker Cahill · Artist Talk + Q&A

Who She Is

Nancy Baker Cahill is a self-taught artist. She admitted being self-conscious about her work around traditional artists — that vulnerability stood out immediately. She didn't come from a formal art background, yet she now works at the intersection of VR, AR, drawing, and sculpture.

Her Creative Journey

  • Started with graphite drawing — that is where all her work begins, even now.
  • Transitioned into VR (Virtual Reality) art, using rendering and 3D tools.
  • Became a sculptural artist, but translates her VR work into physical space using photogrammetry (capturing real objects into 3D models).
  • Uses projection mapping and inverted AR images — creating work that exists in two spaces at once: invisible and visible.

Key Concepts & Techniques

  • Neuroscience of VR art: She discussed how creating in VR affects the brain differently than traditional media — the spatial awareness, the full-body engagement.
  • Multi-spatial art: Her work exists across physical, digital, and augmented realities.
  • AR (Augmented Reality): She described it as operating in two spaces at once — invisible and visible — drawing a connection to quantum mechanics.
  • Auditory + active movement: Her art feels very spatial and immersive; the viewer moves through it.
  • VFX and distortions: She manipulates perception intentionally.
  • Anti-gatekeeping: She created an open-source 4th wall AI platform to democratize access to AR tools.

Ongoing Projects

  • Oceans and AI course at Harvard — an ongoing project exploring ecological awareness through technology.
  • Ecological themes: LA wildfires, ocean conservation — she has a message she wants to convey, and she conveys it hard.
  • Civic engagement is central to her practice — art as activism.

Design Principles Observed

scale (BIG) distortion contrast (visible/invisible) space (multi-spatial) movement immersive experience

Scale is a major principle she uses — her work commands physical and digital space. She thinks analytically, calling herself a trans-disciplinary artist — no separation between art, science, technology, or activism. She is constantly making work, coming up with new ideas, and securing funding to keep pushing boundaries.

Page Two · Reflection · What It Means to Me

Connection to Class Material

Nancy Baker Cahill's work connects directly to several topics we covered in MEDPL 150:

  • New Media Art: She is a perfect example of an artist using emerging media (VR, AR, projection mapping) as her primary medium.
  • Design Principles in Digital Space: Scale, contrast, and spatial relationships function differently in VR/AR than in physical sculpture. She manipulates distortion and perspective in ways impossible with traditional media.
  • User Experience / Immersion: Her AR works require the viewer to move through space, engaging with art actively rather than passively. This mirrors our discussions about interactive design and how audiences experience digital interfaces.
  • Visual Communication with Purpose: She uses her platform for civic engagement and ecological awareness — art as a tool for change, not just decoration.
  • The Fourth Wall & Access: Her anti-gatekeeping platform aligns with our class conversations about making digital tools accessible. She gives away the means of production.

My Personal Takeaway

I respect the artist because I went on a similar journey — but I don't resonate with the work.

Like Nancy, I started with traditional drawing. Graphite, illustration, hand-drawn animation — that tactile, frame-by-frame process is where my heart lives. I also felt insecure around traditional artists, wondering if my work was "serious enough." Seeing her succeed as a self-taught artist is affirming. It proves that you don't need a pedigree to make meaningful, technically sophisticated art.

However, her work itself doesn't land for me emotionally. I understand it intellectually — the quantum mechanics reference, the multi-spatial existence, the ecological messaging — but I don't feel it. Her art is analytical, cerebral, and conceptually dense. That's valid. But I lean toward visceral, narrative, character-driven work. I want to see a face, read an expression, feel a story unfold frame by frame. Her work is about space and perception. Mine is about people and emotion.

What I do admire is her fearlessness. She doesn't wait for permission. She learns the tools, builds the platforms, and makes the work on her own terms. That is something I want to carry forward — not her aesthetic, but her approach.

Conclusion

Nancy Baker Cahill is a trans-disciplinary force. She merges art, neuroscience, environmental activism, and emerging technology into immersive experiences. I respect her journey, her analytical rigor, and her commitment to anti-gatekeeping. Her work taught me that scale and spatial awareness can be design principles just as powerful as color or composition. Even if I don't connect with her work emotionally, I learned from her process: make the work, keep learning, and don't let tradition dictate what art can be.
