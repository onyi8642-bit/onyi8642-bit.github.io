The Middle Passage

Artist: Onyinyechi Erondu (me)
Year: 2018-2019
Inspiration: The Middle Passage by Tom Feelings

This is a bust with additional elements — a statement on civil rights movements. I made it to show the severity of the impacts of the slave trade.

Historical Context · The Middle Passage

The Middle Passage was the central leg of the Triangular Trade — a massive shipping circuit that fueled the economies of Europe, Africa, and the Americas for over three centuries.

📊 Between 1500 and 1866: ~12.5 million Africans transported
💔 ~10.7 million survived the journey
⚰️ Estimated 15% to 25% mortality rate on board

The voyage could take anywhere from three weeks to several months. My sculpture confronts this history directly.

Materials & Scale

Materials: Clay for the bust and ocean body. The ship is made with toothpicks and medical tape.
Scale: Bust-sized with extended elements — the ship rises above, while three mini figures struggle in the ceramic ocean.

⚒️ Technical Methods: Hand-built clay sculpture with assembled ship. The rough texture of the ocean was intentional — slaves fighting the current.

Design Principles · Balance & Form

Balance / Symmetry: Most busts are innately symmetrical, but I wouldn't call this a balanced piece. The way the ship falls makes this asymmetrical — the weight leans to one side, creating tension.

Form / Shape: Organic, not geometric — human form + body of water. The curves of the face contrast with the angular, fragile ship.

Repetition & Texture

Repetition: I have three mini slaves struggling in the ceramic ocean. Their poses echo each other, reinforcing the collective suffering.

Texture: The ocean body could be smooth, but I made it rough — it made sense since the slaves are fighting the current. The contrast between rough water and smooth skin adds visual and tactile tension.

Contrast & Space

Contrast: This is a very dark piece. I used a lot of black and dark blue to show the severity. Any use of white — eyes, tears, the sails on the ship — really illuminates that darkness.

Space: My interpretation: negative space means something has to be bare. Since I'm a maximalist, I only see positive space in this sculpture — every inch is filled with meaning and suffering.

Proportion & Hierarchy

Proportion: As mentioned, this is an unbalanced piece, so proportion is in play. Four slaves total, but one is marginally bigger than the rest — that plays into hierarchy. For some reason I forget that the main slave is drowning too.

👁️ Focal Point: The main figure's eyes draw you in. The tears and the white sails create visual anchors.

Unity & Variety

Unity: All the elements — bust, ship, ocean, mini figures — display a cohesive artwork. The dark palette and shared theme hold everything together.

Variety: What adds visual interest or tension? The contrast and tension between the ship and the main slave — one above, one below; one wooden and fragile, one organic and drowning.

Influence · Tom Feelings

Tom Feelings was an American artist, cartoonist, children's book illustrator, author, teacher, and activist. Many of his books focus on African-American and African culture, including To Be a Slave, Jambo Means Hello, and The Middle Passage: White Ships/Black Cargo.

📷 reference — Tom Feelings artwork

His graphic novel The Middle Passage inspired my sculpture. I wanted to translate his two-dimensional grief into a three-dimensional, tactile form.

Maximalism vs Minimalism

When it comes to design principles, I think it's interesting: one screams the principles loudly and the other whispers them.

Minimalism screams — clean lines, empty space, every element has to fight for its place.
Maximalism whispers — abundance, layering, emotion over emptiness. My sculpture is maximalist. It doesn't whisper; it drowns you in detail.

Conclusion · What I Learned

This sculpture taught me that art can hold history without flattening it. Every rough texture, every dark glaze, every asymmetrical choice — they all serve the story. The Middle Passage cannot be reduced to statistics. My job as an artist is to make people feel what those numbers mean.

If I made it today, I would push the contrast even further — more white against the black, more visible tears. But the core remains: the ship above, the drowning below, and the silence in between.
