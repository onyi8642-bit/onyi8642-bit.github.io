Reading 2

Reflection: While I am not "pro-AI," I recognize the pragmatic reality that this technology is not going anywhere anytime soon. However, the conversation often focuses too heavily on the software itself, when it is actually the users whom we should be wary of. Like any significant technological leap, AI acts as a double-edged sword: it is a "tool" that can either help a person reach their destination faster or effectively stunt their intellectual and creative growth.

There is a beautiful potential in AI to act as a bridge for those who may not be naturally artistically gifted, allowing them to finally externalize the complex visions held in their heads. Yet, this potential is currently being overshadowed by the rise of "slop." As Nesrine Malik argues in The Guardian, this influx of low-quality, AI-generated filler is doing more than just cluttering our digital feeds; it is distorting our collective reality. By prioritizing speed and volume over substance, we risk "sleepwalking into a disaster" where human intent is lost to a sea of algorithmic noise.

The danger lies in the user’s choice: to use AI as a catalyst for genuine expression or as a shortcut that bypasses the discipline required for growth. If we treat AI as a replacement for effort rather than a supplement to it, we contribute to the very "slop" that devalues human creativity. The tool is here to stay, but if we aren't careful, we may find that in our rush to produce more, we have actually learned to think and feel less.
